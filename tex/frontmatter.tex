% ---------------------------------------------------------------- ABSTRACT
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

This thesis addresses the critical issue of railway ballast degradation, which significantly impacts the safety, stability, and operational efficiency of railway tracks. Tailored specifically to the unique conditions of Indian Railways, the research aims to develop a deeper understanding of ballast behaviour under impact loading and to quantify the magnitude of ballast degradation using three-dimensional image processing.

At the core of the study is the modification of the European-standard drop-weight impact-test apparatus to better align with Indian Railways ballast specifications and operational conditions. The experimental set-up is calibrated so that it accurately replicates the on-field stress conditions experienced by ballast in the Indian context, providing a more relevant and reliable framework for analysis. The modified apparatus serves as a critical tool for evaluating ballast degradation under controlled yet realistic conditions.

A significant innovation in this study is the use of advanced three-dimensional image-processing techniques to evaluate morphological changes in ballast particles before and after impact testing. High-resolution structured-light scans are captured to analyse changes in particle shape, size, and texture, providing a detailed and quantitative understanding of ballast degradation. This paired before--after approach allows precise measurement of deterioration, enabling the identification of critical thresholds and patterns of degradation that are not easily detectable through traditional methods.

The outcomes of this study advance railroad-ballast degradation assessment through image-based morphology, delivering a scalable and accurate complement to conventional physical testing, with substantial implications for railway safety and maintenance.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} railway ballast; drop-weight impact loading; Indian Railways; fouling index; structured-light scanning; three-dimensional particle morphology; under-ballast mat.
